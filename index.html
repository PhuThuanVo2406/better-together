[ 07 — Call for abstracts ]

Your research. Your perspective. Your voice.

The Phi Theta Kappa Omega Sigma Chapter invites Houston City College students to submit their research for Better Together: A Student Conference on Belonging and Human Connection.

All disciplines welcome

We welcome research from all academic disciplines, including psychology, sociology, education, business, technology, health sciences, humanities, communication, and more. Whether your research examines a social challenge, investigates human behavior, evaluates a program, or explores an innovative solution, we want to hear your perspective.

First-time researchers welcome

You do not need to be an experienced researcher to participate. Research developed through a class, an Honors project, or an independent study is welcome. There is no single question you must answer. Choose a topic that interests you, investigate it using evidence, and share what you discover.

Suggested research themes

These themes are intended to inspire your research, not limit it. You are encouraged to develop your own research question and explore any related topic that interests you.

01

Psychology, Loneliness & Mental Well-Being

02

Student Life, Belonging & Campus Communities

03

Technology, Social Media & Human Connection

04

Culture, Identity & Social Inclusion

05

Education, Collaboration & Student Success

06

Leadership, Community Engagement & Social Impact

07

Innovative Solutions & Building a More Connected Future

+

Have another idea? We welcome it!

These themes are suggestions, not restrictions. Your research may combine multiple disciplines, explore a different perspective, or investigate an issue not explicitly listed above. As long as your work has a meaningful connection to the conference's central themes of loneliness, belonging, or human connection, we encourage you to submit. We value curiosity, originality, thoughtful analysis, and diverse perspectives.

Abstract submission guidelines

Eligibility
  • Open to currently enrolled Houston City College students. Phi Theta Kappa membership is not required.
  • Presentations may include research conducted as part of coursework, Honors projects, independent studies, or other academic activities.
  • Under the current conference format, research teams must consist of 2–5 students and identify one team leader as the primary contact.
  • Each team must have a faculty sponsor who reviews the abstract before submission.
Abstract requirements

Abstracts must be 150–250 words and include:

  • Research purpose: the topic, objective, or central question investigated.
  • Methodology: how the research was conducted, including relevant data, sources, or analytical approaches.
  • Key findings: the principal results, discoveries, or insights.
  • Significance: why the findings matter and how they relate to the conference themes.

Your abstract should be clear, concise, and understandable to an interdisciplinary audience.

Research requirements
  1. Completed research only. Your project must be substantially completed, with findings available, before you submit your abstract. Research proposals or projects without results will not be considered.
  2. Original conference presentation. Your work must not have been previously presented at another academic conference.
  3. Class projects are welcome. Research completed for a class is eligible, provided it is adapted for a conference presentation.
  4. Academic integrity. All submissions must represent the researchers' own work, appropriately acknowledge sources, and accurately report methods and findings.
  5. AI usage. Abstracts must be written by the student researchers without generative AI assistance, including AI-powered rewriting or grammar-generation tools such as Grammarly's AI features. Basic, non-generative spelling and grammar checks are permitted. Presentations should likewise reflect students' own writing, analysis, and preparation. Research examining AI as a subject is welcome.

Presentation formats

01Oral

Oral research presentation

  • 10-minute presentation followed by 5 minutes of audience questions.
  • Four presentation slots are currently planned.
  • Presenters should communicate their findings clearly and prepare appropriate visual materials.
02Poster

Poster presentation

  • An opportunity to discuss research findings directly with attendees in an interactive setting.
  • Limited to 20 accepted submissions.
  • Accepted presenters are responsible for preparing, printing, and bringing their own physical research posters.
Presenter responsibilities

Submitting an abstract represents a commitment to present if selected.

  • At least two members of each team must participate as speakers during an oral presentation.
  • All accepted presenters must be available on the conference date, Saturday, February 13, 2027.
  • Teams must monitor their student email for acceptance notifications and conference instructions.
  • If unexpected circumstances prevent participation, the organizing committee must be notified as soon as possible.

Please submit only if you are prepared to present your research if accepted.

[ 09 — Questions ]

Good to know

Who can attend?

All Houston City College students are welcome. Commuter and working students are especially encouraged to come.

Do I need to be a Phi Theta Kappa member?

No. Phi Theta Kappa membership is not required to attend or to present.

Can I present on my own?

Under the current conference format, research teams must have 2–5 students, with one team leader as the primary contact and a faculty sponsor who reviews the abstract.

Do I have to talk to strangers?

No. Every Connection Round is voluntary, and you can sit out and stay in the room.

Why are licensed professionals involved?

Loneliness often overlaps with anxiety or depression, so trained counselors lead the breakouts to keep the event safe and supportive.

Who do I contact with questions?

Email Chapter President Vincent Vo at vop.president.omegasigma@outlook.com for any conference information. Happy to help.
