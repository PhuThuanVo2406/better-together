Why we're doing this

Loneliness is common. Connection can be designed.

Better Together is the action of our Honors in Action project. Our research asked how community colleges can design interventions that increase meaningful interaction between students. The answer pointed to structured, face-to-face time, so that is what this day is built around.

1 in 6people worldwide experience lonelinessWHO Commission on Social Connection
25%of college students report moderate to severe lonelinessTrellis Strategies, 2026
30%of students who drop out name feeling isolated as a reasonTrellis Strategies

Goals for the day

  1. Raise awareness of loneliness among commuter and working students.
  2. Give students a low-pressure way to meet peers face to face.
  3. Give student research teams a stage to present their work.
  4. Connect students with licensed professionals and campus resources.
  5. Measure the change in belonging with a short feedback survey.

How Connection Rounds work

Each pair gets a prompt card and 10 minutes. A bell marks the switch to a new partner. Phones go face down. Anyone can sit out a round and still stay in the room.

Round 1 · lightWhat's one thing you wish people knew about being a student here?
Round 1 · deeperWhat's something you're working on that you're proud of?
Round 2 · after todayWhat's one way we could keep in touch?
Call for abstracts

Form a team. Pick a question. Present it.

Four presentation slots are open in the 12:15 research block. Choose a topic from the list below or propose your own, as long as it connects to loneliness, belonging or student connection.

2–5students per team
  • Solo entries and teams larger than 5 are not accepted.
  • All members are current HCC students. PTK membership is not required.
  • Name one team lead as the contact person.
  • Get a faculty sponsor to read your abstract.
  • Give every member a role. At least two members speak.
  • No team yet? Contact the chapter officers and we'll match you.

What to submit

  1. Project title and topic number (or "own topic")
  2. Team name, team lead, every member's name and student email
  3. Faculty sponsor name and department
  4. An abstract of 150–250 words: question, approach, main takeaway
  5. Format: 10-minute talk with 5 minutes of questions

Research topics

20 questions worth answering

Each topic comes with a suggested way to study it at HCC. Put the topic number on your submission.

Theme A

Psychology of loneliness

  1. 01
    What is the "liking gap," and does it stop students from starting conversations?Literature review plus a survey after a conversation activity
  2. 02
    How is loneliness different from being alone, and why does it matter?Review of emotional vs. social loneliness
  3. 03
    How do loneliness, anxiety and depression feed each other in college students?Review of national survey data
  4. 04
    Does fear of being judged keep students out of clubs and study groups?Anonymous campus survey
  5. 05
    Why do lonely students avoid asking for help, and what reduces that stigma?Literature review plus counselor interviews
Theme B

Commuter and working students

  1. 06
    How do commuting and work schedules limit friendships at community college?Survey of HCC commuter and working students
  2. 07
    Do online and hybrid classes increase or reduce loneliness?Compare online and in-person survey answers
  3. 08
    What blocks international and multilingual students from making friends?Interviews or a focus group
  4. 09
    How do student parents and adult learners find community on campus?Interviews plus a review of campus services
  5. 10
    Which campus spaces and times actually work for busy students to meet?Map spaces and survey when students are on campus
Theme C

Technology and connection

  1. 11
    Can AI chatbot companions reduce loneliness, or do they replace human contact?Review of recent studies
  2. 12
    Does a phone on the table lower the quality of a face-to-face conversation?Small experiment with and without phones
  3. 13
    Does cutting back on social media reduce loneliness?One-week challenge with a before/after survey
  4. 14
    Do class group chats and Discord servers build real friendships?Survey plus a review of class chats, with permission
  5. 15

Built on evidence

The research behind the design

Friesen, 2024

Conferences build belonging

Community college students at an academic conference grew closer, valued their college more and felt they belonged. Faculty acted as the catalyst.

Ellard et al., 2023

Small groups work

12 of 14 interventions built on support groups, interaction or reflection reduced loneliness, versus 4 of 10 talk-only ones.

Sharp et al., 2006

Awareness opens doors

A 40-minute session on mental-health myths and treatment improved attitudes toward seeking help for up to a month.

Questions

Good to know

Who can attend?

All HCC students, free of charge. Commuter and working students are especially welcome.

Do I have to talk to strangers?

No. Every Connection Round is voluntary, and you can sit out and stay in the room.

Can I present on my own?

No. Presentations are by teams of 2 to 5. Contact the officers if you need to be matched with a team.

Why are licensed professionals involved?

Loneliness often overlaps with anxiety or depression, so trained counselors lead the breakouts to keep the event safe.

How do I stay updated?

Follow @omega.sigma.ptk on Instagram or come to the next chapter general meeting.
